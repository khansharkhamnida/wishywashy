🧺 iOS app · Singapore

Never find a full laundromat again.

WishyWashy lets you find, reserve and pay for a washer or dryer at self-service laundromats, then tells you when to leave so you arrive on time. Three mini games keep you busy while you wait.

See the features View on GitHub

How it works

You pay when you check in, not when you reserve. Cancel for free any time before that.

1

Find a machine

Pick a laundromat and see which washers and dryers are free right now.

2

Reserve it

Book Now or Later. You get 10 minutes after the start time to check in.

3

Arrive on time

The app works out when to leave and can remind you.

4

Check in & pay

Pay at the machine, watch the cycle run, and play a game while you wait.

Features

Everything works today in the app, running on sample data.

🏠

Home

  • Cartoon washing machines with bobbing bubbles in the porthole
  • Availability, type, capacity, price and time until free
  • Open / closed status and a quick Change location link
  • Reserve Now or Later in 30 minute slots, 7 AM to 11 PM
📅

Smart booking rules

  • A busy machine that frees up within 15 minutes can be reserved
  • 10 minute check-in window, then the machine is released
  • Up to 5 upcoming bookings, one waiting for check-in
  • Overlaps and too-close slots are never offered
🧼

My Laundry

  • Live view of what is running and what is next
  • Countdown on your reserved machine
  • Progress bars and time left for running cycles
  • Free cancel, and check-in waits if the last user is still there
💳

Pay at check-in

  • Cards, PayNow and Apple Pay in a built-in test sheet
  • The cycle only starts once payment succeeds
  • Automatic refund if the machine is lost while paying
  • No real money moves yet
⏰

Arrive on time

  • Travel time by car, on foot or public transport (Apple Maps)
  • Leave-by time with a 10 minute safety buffer
  • Tells you if you are fine, should leave now, or would miss it
  • Remind me to leave notification, and a manual mode if you deny permissions

Under the hood

The interesting bits, in plain words first and the proper jargon underneath.

Games

GameplayKit

Apple's toolbox for game brains. It decides what the bubbles and socks do, so the games feel alive and fair.

  • Bubbles that don't bunch up. Each bubble steers itself and keeps its distance from the others. GKAgent2D + GKGoal
  • A game that notices how you're doing. Playing well makes it faster, struggling makes it chill out. GKRuleSystem (fuzzy logic)
  • Knowing if the game is waiting, playing or over. GKStateMachine
  • Fair shuffling. Colours take turns instead of repeating, and bubble sizes cluster around "medium". GKShuffledDistribution, GKGaussianDistribution
  • Same daily puzzle for everyone. A "seed" number deals the same Sock Match deck to all players that day. GKMersenneTwisterRandomSource
  • Lego-style game objects. A bubble is an empty shell with pieces clipped on: looks, movement, score. GKEntity + GKComponentSystem
Getting there

MapKit & Core Location

Works out how long it takes you to reach the laundromat, so you know when to leave.

  • Finds laundromats near you and estimates the trip by car, on foot or public transport. MKLocalSearch, MKDirections
  • Asks where you are once, not constantly tracking you. CLLocationManager
Reminders

UserNotifications

A nudge on your phone that says "time to leave" before your booking starts. Recalculating replaces it, and cancelling removes it.

  • Local notifications scheduled on the device, so no server is needed. UNUserNotificationCenter
Architecture

VIPER & protocols

Every screen is split into small jobs: one part draws, one decides, one fetches, one moves you to the next screen. The app also talks to a "booking desk" through a contract, so the fake desk on your phone can be swapped for the real one without touching a single screen.

  • VIPER: View, Interactor, Presenter, Entity, Router
  • Protocol-oriented services: BookingService, PaymentService, AuthService with a local and a Supabase version
  • Dependency injection: tests can plug in stand-ins
Backend

Supabase & Postgres

The online database and the "rulebook". The rules live inside the database, so nobody can skip them, not even by changing their phone's clock.

  • Row-level security (RLS): you can only see your own bookings, like a locker that only opens for you.
  • Database functions (RPC): book_machine, cancel_booking, check_in do all the booking and use the server's clock.
  • No double booking: a constraint makes the database refuse two reservations on one machine at the same time, even if two people tap together.
  • Triggers: sign up and a profile is created automatically.
  • pg_cron: a clock that ticks every minute to expire old holds and free machines.
  • Rules as data: numbers like the 10 minute window sit in a table, so they can change without a new app release.
Accounts

Supabase Auth & Keychain

Sign in without the app ever storing your password. The sign-in tokens live in the phone's safe.

  • Keychain holds the access and refresh tokens, never UserDefaults.
  • Plain URLSession calls Supabase, with no extra package to install.
  • Opt-in live mode: off by default, so the sample app still runs on its own.
Safety net

Automated tests

Little robots that try to break the rules every time the code changes.

  • Swift unit tests for the booking rules (expiry, overlaps, limits).
  • 32 SQL tests run on a throwaway Postgres, checking the rules, time passing and security.
